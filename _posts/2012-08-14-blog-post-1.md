## 1. Introduction & Motivation

In private machine learning, **Noisy Stochastic Gradient Descent** (also referred to as **DP-SGD** or **Noisy-SGD**) stands as the fundamental workhorse algorithm[cite: 5, 252, 256]. The procedure modifies standard SGD by clipping per-sample gradients to a bounded norm and injecting Gaussian noise into minibatch gradient updates at every step[cite: 6, 253].

Historically, privacy analyses of Noisy-SGD rely on **composition theorems**[cite: 11, 258]. In the **Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP)** framework introduced by Mironov (2017) [cite: 12, 259, 121], each step is viewed as a separate privacy-releasing mechanism[cite: 11, 258]. Under standard privacy amplification by subsampling and composition (Abadi et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2019)[cite: 13, 261, 314, 112, 125], running $T$ iterations on a dataset of size $n$ with minibatch size $b$, gradient Lipschitz constant $L$, and noise scale $\sigma$ yields an RDP upper bound of the form:

$$\epsilon_{\text{RDP}}(T) \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma^2} T$$

### The Theoretical Paradox
This classical bound grows **linearly with the number of iterations $T$**[cite: 12, 260, 261]. It implies an unsettling paradox: *every additional training step continuously burns through the privacy budget, even if the optimization trajectory has long since converged to a stationary state*[cite: 14, 262].

However, as shown by Altschuler and Talwar (2023), this unbounded linear growth is an artifact of treating the dynamics as $T$ independent releases[cite: 15, 28, 263, 308]. Noisy-SGD is **not** merely a sequence of independent disclosures; it is a **noisy contractive dynamical system**[cite: 28, 101, 308, 534].

When loss functions are $M$-smooth and convex, and updates are projected onto a closed convex domain $K$ of diameter $D$ [cite: 48, 264, 341], the update operator is non-expansive[cite: 29, 57, 309, 383]. Coupled with Gaussian noise injection at every iteration, the system exhibits **privacy amplification by iteration (PABI)** [cite: 30, 310]: *the state space continually loses memory of initial condition discrepancies*[cite: 31, 102, 311, 535].

This blog post provides a mathematical exploration of **privacy saturation**[cite: 23, 301], proving that the privacy loss of Noisy-SGD flattens out after a characteristic burn-in time scale:

$$\bar{T} \approx \frac{Dn}{L\eta}$$

## 2. Problem Setup & Mathematical Definitions

Consider two adjacent datasets $X = \{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ and $X' = \{x_1', \dots, x_n'\}$ in a domain $\mathcal{X}^n$ that differ in exactly one entry, say at index $i^*$[cite: 45, 46, 333, 336, 337].

### Domain and Function Assumptions
1. **Feasible Domain $K$**: Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be a closed convex set with bounded diameter:
   $$D = \text{diam}(K) = \sup_{u, v \in K} \|u - v\|_2 < \infty$$
2. **Loss Functions**: For each data point $x_i$, the associated loss $f_i: K \to \mathbb{R}$ is convex, $L$-Lipschitz ($|f_i(u) - f_i(v)| \le L\|u-v\|$), and $M$-smooth ($\|\nabla f_i(u) - \nabla f_i(v)\| \le M\|u-v\|$) on $K$[cite: 48, 341].

### Algorithm: Projected Noisy-SGD
Starting from an arbitrary initialization $W_0 = W_0' = w_0 \in K$ [cite: 48, 86, 343, 447], at iteration $t \in \{0, \dots, T-1\}$, a minibatch $B_t \subset [n]$ of size $b$ is sampled uniformly at random[cite: 48, 344]. The update evolves as:

$$W_{t+1} = \Pi_K \left[ W_t - \frac{\eta}{b} \sum_{i \in B_t} \nabla f_i(W_t) + \eta Z_t \right], \quad Z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_d)$$

where $\Pi_K$ denotes Euclidean projection onto $K$ [cite: 48, 346, 350], and $\eta > 0$ is the step size[cite: 48, 350]. The noise added directly to the iterate has covariance $\eta^2 \sigma^2 I_d$[cite: 48, 351, 352].

### Privacy Guarantees
We measure privacy using Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP)[cite: 54, 56, 373, 376].

* **Definition (Rényi Divergence)**: For probability distributions $P$ and $Q$ with densities $p$ and $q$, the Rényi divergence of order $\alpha > 1$ is defined as[cite: 55, 56, 374, 375]:
  $$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P \| Q) = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1} \log \int p(x)^\alpha q(x)^{1-\alpha} \, dx$$
* **Definition (RDP)**: An algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ satisfies $(\alpha, \epsilon)$-RDP if $\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{\mathcal{A}(X)} \| P_{\mathcal{A}(X')}) \le \epsilon$ for all adjacent $X, X'$[cite: 56, 376, 377].

## 3. Preliminary Lemmas & Optimal Transport Foundation

Understanding why privacy saturates requires combining two distinct concepts: **statistical distinguishability** (measured by Rényi divergence) [cite: 55, 374] and **geometric state separation** (measured via Optimal Transport and $W_\infty$ distance)[cite: 66, 70, 74, 410, 419, 429].

### Geometric Contraction & Divergence Properties

* **Lemma 1 (Gradient Step Contraction)**: If $f$ is convex and $M$-smooth, then for any step size $\eta \le 2/M$, the gradient mapping $u \mapsto u - \eta \nabla f(u)$ is non-expansive[cite: 57, 382, 383]:
  $$\|(u - \eta \nabla f(u)) - (v - \eta \nabla f(v))\| \le \|u - v\|$$
  Since projection $\Pi_K$ onto a convex set is non-expansive ($\|\Pi_K(u) - \Pi_K(v)\| \le \|u-v\|$), the entire update step is non-expansive[cite: 57, 384, 385, 386].

* **Lemma 2 (Post-Processing & Composition)**:
  * *Post-Processing*: $\mathcal{D}_\alpha(h_\# \mu \| h_\# \nu) \le \mathcal{D}_\alpha(\mu \| \nu)$ for any deterministic or randomized mapping $h$[cite: 58, 387, 388].
  * *Conditional Composition*: For sequence of random variables $(X_1, \dots, X_k)$ and $(Y_1, \dots, Y_k)$[cite: 59, 391, 393]:
    $$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{X_{1:k}} \| P_{Y_{1:k}}) \le \sum_{i=1}^k \sup_{x_{<i}} \mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{X_i | X_{<i} = x_{<i}} \| P_{Y_i | Y_{<i} = x_{<i}})$$

### The Optimal Transport Perspective: $W_\infty$ Distance & Shifted RDP

To track how geometric contraction reduces privacy loss over time, we introduce the $\infty$-Wasserstein distance $W_\infty$ and **Shifted Rényi Divergence**[cite: 70, 75, 76, 419, 430, 431].

* **Definition ($W_\infty$ Distance)**: For distributions $\mu, \nu$, the $W_\infty$ metric measures the essential supremum distance under optimal coupling[cite: 70, 419]:
  $$W_\infty(\mu, \nu) = \inf_{\pi \in \Pi(\mu, \nu)} \text{ess\,sup}_{(X,Y) \sim \pi} \|X - Y\|_2$$
  If $W_\infty(\mu, \nu) \le z$, then $\mu$ and $\nu$ can be coupled such that their realizations are almost surely within Euclidean distance $z$[cite: 70, 420].

* **Definition (Shifted Rényi Divergence)**: For $z \ge 0$, the $z$-shifted Rényi divergence is defined as[cite: 76, 431]:
  $$\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(z)}(\mu \| \nu) = \inf_{\tilde{\mu}: W_\infty(\tilde{\mu}, \mu) \le z} \mathcal{D}_\alpha(\tilde{\mu} \| \nu)$$
  *Interpretation*: Before evaluating statistical divergence, we are allowed to shift distribution $\mu$ by a geometric distance of up to $z$[cite: 76, 433].

* **Lemma 3 (Shift-Reduction for Gaussian Noise)**: For any distributions $\mu, \nu$ and shift $a \ge 0$[cite: 78, 79, 436, 437]:
  $$\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(z)}(\mu * \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_d) \| \nu * \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_d)) \le \mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(z+a)}(\mu \| \nu) + \frac{\alpha a^2}{2\sigma^2}$$

* **Lemma 4 (Contraction-Reduction)**: Let $\phi, \phi'$ be coupled contractive random functions with $\sup_x \|\phi(x) - \phi'(x)\| \le s$ almost surely[cite: 80, 81, 438, 439]. Then[cite: 82, 441]:
  $$\mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(z+s)}(\phi_\# \mu \| \phi'_\# \mu') \le \mathcal{D}_\alpha^{(z)}(\mu \| \mu')$$

## 4. Main Theorem & Formal Proof

### Theorem 1 (Privacy Saturation of Noisy-SGD)
Under the setup in Section 2, if the step size satisfies $\eta \le 2/M$, then for any total number of iterations $T \ge 1$, projected Noisy-SGD satisfies[cite: 83, 84, 443, 444, 445]:

$$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{W_T} \| P_{W_T'}) \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma^2} \min \left\{ T, \left\lceil \frac{Dn}{L\eta} \right\rceil \right\}$$

---

### Complete Proof Walkthrough

#### Step 1: Noise Splitting Trick
Let $\{W_t\}_{t=0}^T$ and $\{W_t'\}_{t=0}^T$ be trajectories produced on adjacent datasets $X, X'$ using identical initializations $w_0$ and identical minibatch sequences $B_0, \dots, B_{T-1}$[cite: 86, 447].

Decompose the Gaussian noise variance $\sigma^2$ into two independent components[cite: 86, 449]:
$$\sigma^2 = \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2$$

Assign:
* $Y_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \eta^2 \sigma_1^2 I_d)$: reserved for driving the **iteration-forgetting mechanism**[cite: 86, 87, 452, 453, 455].
* $Z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \eta^2 \sigma_2^2 I_d)$: reserved for absorbing the **sampled Gaussian privacy cost**[cite: 86, 87, 452, 453, 454].

The trajectories can be rewritten as[cite: 88, 457]:
$$W_{t+1} = \Pi_K \left[ W_t - \frac{\eta}{b} \sum_{i \in B_t} \nabla f_i(W_t) + Y_t + Z_t \right]$$
$$W_{t+1}' = \Pi_K \left[ W_t' - \frac{\eta}{b} \sum_{i \in B_t} \nabla f_i(W_t') + Y_t + Z_t' \right]$$

where $Z_t' = \tilde{Z}_t + \frac{\eta}{b} \left( \nabla f_{i^*}(W_t') - \nabla f_{i^*}'(W_t') \right) \mathbb{I}_{\{i^* \in B_t\}}$ with $\tilde{Z}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \eta^2 \sigma_2^2 I_d)$[cite: 88, 458, 459].

#### Step 2: Lookback Window $\tau = T - R$
Fix an arbitrary lookback time $\tau \in \{0, \dots, T-1\}$ and let $R = T - \tau$ be the window of final iterations to analyze[cite: 89, 463, 464, 465].

By Post-Processing (Lemma 2) and Conditional Composition (Lemma 2) applied to $(Z_{\tau:T-1}, W_T)$[cite: 90, 467, 468, 471]:
$$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{W_T} \| P_{W_T'}) \le \underbrace{\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{Z_{\tau:T-1}} \| P_{Z_{\tau:T-1}'})}_{\text{Term I: Sampling Cost}} + \underbrace{\sup_z \mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{W_T | Z_{\tau:T-1}=z} \| P_{W_T' | Z_{\tau:T-1}'=z})}_{\text{Term II: Iteration Forgetting Cost}}$$

#### Step 3: Bounding Term I (Sampling Cost)
Using Subsampled Gaussian Mechanism bounds over the $R$ steps[cite: 91, 94, 478, 484]:
* The changed item $i^*$ enters $B_t$ with probability $q = b/n$[cite: 94, 483].
* The maximum gradient shift induced by the single changed point is $m_t \le \frac{2\eta L}{b}$[cite: 93, 482].

Applying the subsampled RDP lemma over $R$ steps[cite: 94, 484, 487]:
$$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{Z_{\tau:T-1}} \| P_{Z_{\tau:T-1}'}) \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma_2^2} R$$

#### Step 4: Bounding Term II (Iteration Forgetting Cost)
Condition on $Z_{\tau:T-1} = Z_{\tau:T-1}' = z$[cite: 95, 488, 489]. Since $W_\tau, W_\tau' \in K$ and $\text{diam}(K) = D$, the maximum initial distance at time $\tau$ satisfies[cite: 97, 496]:

$$W_\infty(P_{W_\tau}, P_{W_\tau'}) \le D$$

Applying the PABI bound (Lemma 3 & 4) across the $R$ contractive steps with step allocations $a_t = D/R$[cite: 97, 498, 500]:
$$\sup_z \mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{W_T | Z_{\tau:T-1}=z} \| P_{W_T' | Z_{\tau:T-1}'=z}) \le \frac{\alpha D^2}{2 \eta^2 \sigma_1^2 R}$$

#### Step 5: Trade-off Optimization & Burn-In Scale
Combining both terms[cite: 98, 504]:
$$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{W_T} \| P_{W_T'}) \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma_2^2} R + \frac{\alpha D^2}{2 \eta^2 \sigma_1^2 R}$$

Setting equal noise splits $\sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2 = \sigma^2 / 2$[cite: 98, 516]:
$$F(R) = \frac{2\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma^2} R + \frac{\alpha D^2}{\eta^2 \sigma^2 R}$$

Minimizing $F(R)$ with respect to $R$ gives the optimal window size $R^*$[cite: 98, 510, 511, 512, 515]:
$$\frac{dF}{dR} = 0 \implies R^* = \frac{Dn}{L\eta}$$

* If $T \le \bar{T} = \left\lceil \frac{Dn}{L\eta} \right\rceil$, evaluate composition from $t = 0$ ($R = T$), yielding $\mathcal{D}_\alpha \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma^2} T$[cite: 98, 517, 519].
* If $T > \bar{T}$, set $\tau = T - \bar{T}$ ($R = \bar{T}$), yielding $\mathcal{D}_\alpha \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma^2} \bar{T}$[cite: 98, 520, 526].

Combining both regimes yields the final saturation bound[cite: 98, 527, 529]:
$$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{W_T} \| P_{W_T'}) \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma^2} \min \left\{ T, \left\lceil \frac{Dn}{L\eta} \right\rceil \right\} \quad \blacksquare$$

## 5. Summary & Key Takeaways
1. **Dynamics Matter**: Privacy loss does not accumulate indefinitely in noisy contractive optimization systems[cite: 23, 28, 101, 301, 534].
2. **Optimal Transport Bridge**: The $W_\infty$ metric and shifted Rényi divergence provide the mathematical tools needed to isolate geometric state contraction from statistical divergence[cite: 70, 75, 76, 104, 105, 419, 430, 431, 537, 538].
3. **Practical Consequence**: You can train DP-SGD models for significantly more epochs until convergence without suffering linear privacy degradation, provided the domain is bounded[cite: 16, 23, 111, 265, 301].
