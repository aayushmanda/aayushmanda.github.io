Privacy Saturation in Noisy-SGD

August 17, 2026

Noisy stochastic gradient descent, also known as DP-SGD or Noisy-SGD, modifies standard SGD by clipping per-sample gradients and adding Gaussian noise to minibatch updates.

Classical privacy analyses compose the privacy cost of each step. In the Renyi Differential Privacy framework, this often gives a bound that grows linearly with the number of iterations:

$$\epsilon_{\mathrm{RDP}}(T) \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2 \sigma^2}T.$$

The tension is that a training run can converge while the bound keeps increasing. The privacy saturation view treats Noisy-SGD as a noisy contractive dynamical system rather than a sequence of independent disclosures.

Under convexity, smoothness, and projection to a bounded domain \(K\) of diameter \(D\), the privacy loss can flatten after a burn-in scale on the order of

$$\bar{T} \approx \frac{Dn}{L\eta}.$$

A representative saturation statement is:

$$\mathcal{D}_\alpha(P_{W_T}\|P_{W_T'}) \le \frac{\alpha L^2}{n^2\sigma^2}\min\left\{T,\left\lceil\frac{Dn}{L\eta}\right\rceil\right\}.$$

The key idea is to combine geometric contraction with shifted Renyi divergence. The iterate dynamics continually forget state discrepancies, while injected Gaussian noise limits how distinguishable adjacent trajectories can remain.

Takeaways

  1. Privacy loss need not accumulate indefinitely in noisy contractive optimization systems.
  2. Optimal transport and shifted Renyi divergence help separate geometric contraction from statistical distinguishability.
  3. For bounded domains, DP-SGD can admit iteration-dependent privacy analyses that saturate after a characteristic time scale.
